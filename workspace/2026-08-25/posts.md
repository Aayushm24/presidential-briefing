## OPTION 2, contrarian-philosopher (hook score: 8)

**Conviction:** Garry Tan's "build an AI harness or be replaced" framing is right, but the real question isn't whether to build one, it's whether your team is willing to delete the workflows that made you successful.

**Post:**
Every software company is now racing to add an AI harness.

Almost none are willing to delete the workflow underneath it.

Garry Tan's framing this week was sharp. Build AI solutions on top of the deterministic layers, or get replaced by someone who does. APIs stay. SQL stays. The soft layer above them gets rebuilt.

i think most teams heard the first half and ignored the second.

Adding an AI button on top of your existing product means building decoration. The harness only matters if you're willing to let it eat the workflow the button used to sit on.

Every team i talk to at Atlan realized going AI Native means asking a harder question. Not "how do we add AI to X?" The real question becomes "why does X exist at all?"

One optimizes. One eliminates.

The debate only gets interesting when you're honest about which one you're doing.

Carson's $20K/month Devin bill shows him deleting the engineering process. 15 agents replaced roles. 40 PRs a day close themselves. The harness ate the workflow.

Meanwhile most SaaS teams i see are shipping "AI Assistants" that live next to the workflow they were supposed to replace. Same clicks. Same forms. Same reports. Now with a chat window.

That's decoration, not a harness.

The uncomfortable part IMO: the deterministic layers Tan is pointing at (APIs, SQL, event streams) are already commodities. The competitive advantage was never there. The competitive advantage is in the workflow logic sitting on top, and most companies can't bring themselves to torch it.

What workflow in your product would you delete if AI had always existed?
