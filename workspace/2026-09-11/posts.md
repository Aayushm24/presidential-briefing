## OPTION 3, pattern-observation (hook score: 8, was 7)

**Conviction:** L3: Three timing frameworks (Tunguz's waves, platform distribution capture, AI content economics) all point to the same strategic choice, vertical depth beats horizontal reach

**Post:**
Meta hit No. 2 with Muse. OpenAI paused Pro signups. Pocket FM reached $500M with AI content.

Three separate stories. Same underlying pattern.

Imo the AI market is segmenting into waves, and timing determines everything.

Tomasz Tunguz mapped it well. Wave 1 targets early adopters who change workflows for AI benefits. Wave 2 reaches early majority users who need AI inside existing tools. Wave 3 hits mainstream consumers who expect AI as a default feature.

Meta's Muse represents Wave 3 hitting consumer markets.

When platforms with billions of users ship agent experiences, they compress years of user acquisition into days.

What I keep noticing is that each wave has different competitive dynamics.

Wave 1 companies can win with superior technology because users tolerate complexity for better results.

Wave 2 companies need distribution partnerships with existing enterprise software.

Wave 3 companies compete directly against platform incumbents for mainstream attention.

Pocket FM succeeds because they picked Wave 1 in audio and built vertical depth.

They don't compete against Meta or OpenAI on distribution. They compete against traditional audio companies on production economics.

93% AI-generated content with 80x cost reduction creates a competitive edge platforms can't easily cross.

I build AI agents at Atlan, and we've learned the same lesson.

Our agents work through specialized workflows, GTM research, sales qualification, content generation, rather than general-purpose interfaces.

Tbh, most founders I see build Wave 1 products but target Wave 3 users. That's where timing beats technology.

Wave 1 users accept workflow changes. Wave 2 users need familiar integration. Wave 3 users expect invisible AI.

Which wave is your product actually designed for?
