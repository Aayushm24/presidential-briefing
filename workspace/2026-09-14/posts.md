## OPTION 1, contrarian (hook score: 8)

**Conviction:** L2: Most teams treating fine-tuned models as sovereignty strategies will burn budgets trying to keep up with frontier model improvement velocity.

**Post:**
Fine-tuned small models beat GPT-4 at your specific task today.

Six months later, GPT-5 ships and your specialized model loses.

I see this across the AI teams I talk to. They start with fine-tuned models that clearly outperform available alternatives. Eighteen months later, they're either spending unsustainable amounts on retraining cycles or they've quietly switched back to frontier models with careful prompting strategies.

The math problem is brutal. You train a model that beats the current frontier. The frontier moves up. You retrain to beat the new frontier. The frontier moves again. Each cycle costs more because the target keeps rising.

Meanwhile, frontier model improvements are subsidized by billions in training compute and the best researchers on earth. Your fine-tuning budget competes against that every quarter.

At Atlan, we've learned the teams that survive this recognize early that sovereignty is expensive. They either commit to paying the ongoing cost, quarterly retraining as a permanent line item, or they pivot to strategies that don't depend on model ownership.

Most pivot. Memory and context matter more than model ownership for most applications. Teams that own the retrieval pipeline, the conversation history, and the user feedback loop can build sustainable advantages without owning the underlying model weights.

The real competitive advantages come from building systems that adapt, not systems that optimize.

Are you building a better model, or building a better machine for producing models?
